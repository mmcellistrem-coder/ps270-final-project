Project thoughts

I am interested in exploring data related to the difference presidential parties influence has on the economy. Each president has different implementations that they make during their term and I think gathering data based on the success of each presidential party during from 1961-2024 would be very interesting to compare.

library(readr)

Which presidential party has more positive or more negative impact on the economy during a 60 year period? Typically a republican president would be assumed to make the economy more successful rather than a democratic president. Republican presidents focus more on businesses, deregulation within businesses, tax reduction and energy affordability. These traits of a typical republican president would likely boost the success of the economy. The explanatory variable is whether there was a republican president. I will measure their economic success within their presidential parties. This will be measured by GDP, unemployment rate, inflation rate, misery index, stock market performance, national debt. Greater economic success during the Nixon, Ford, Reagan, George H.W. Bush, George W. Bush, and Trump administrations would prove my thesis correct while greater success within the economy during the Kennedy, Johnson, Carter, Clinton, Obama, and Biden administrations would prove that my hypothesis is wrong.

us_growth <- my_data |>
  filter(`Country Name` == "United States") |>
   pivot_longer(cols = `1961`:`2024`, names_to = "Year", values_to = "Growth") |>
   mutate(year = as.numeric(Year)) |>
   mutate(party = case_when(
    year %in% c(1961:1963, 1963:1969, 1977:1981, 1993:2001, 2009:2017) ~ "Democrat",
    year %in% c(1969:1974, 1974:1977, 1981:1989, 1989:1993, 2001:2009, 2017:2021 ) ~ "Republican",
    )) |>
  filter(party %in% c("Democrat", "Republican")) |>
   filter(!is.na("Other"))

us_growth |>
  group_by(party) |>
  summarize(mean_growth = mean(Growth, na.rm = TRUE)) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = party, y = mean_growth)) +
  geom_col(fill = "grey50", color = "white", width = 0.6) +
  labs(
    title = "Average GDP Growth by Party",
    subtitle = "1961 - 2024",
    x = "Presidential Party",
    y = "Mean Annual GDP Growth(%)"
  ) +
  theme_minimal() +
  theme(legend.position = "none")

Under the Democratic presidents in my data set the average annual GDP growth was 3.29%. To get the average republican growth we would do 3.29 + (-0.551) which would be 2.74. The average growth under a republican president is then 2.74%. The standard error is 0.54 which shows that the variation between years is very high compared to the actual difference between parties. The p-value is 0.318, because this is much higher than 0.05 this means that the result is not statistically significant. There is a 31.8% chance that this difference in GDP growth happened purely by random luck. Because the p-value is greater than 0.05 we fail to reject the null hypothesis, suggesting that the observed difference is due to random changes in the economy occurring rather than it being due to the actual presidency.

The graph shows a variation between republicans and democrats in regards to GDP percent growth. Overall, from the comparison of these presidencies, republican presidents to the democratic presidents, republicans are more likely to boost the economy than democratic presidents are.
